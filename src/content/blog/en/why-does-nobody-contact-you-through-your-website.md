Your website is live, it gets visits, and it even looks good. But the phone never rings, your inbox is empty, and the contact form is gathering dust. If that sounds familiar, you are not alone: it is one of the most common problems on small business websites.

Here is the important part: when you have traffic but zero enquiries, the problem is almost never a lack of visitors. It is what happens after someone arrives on your page. Let us go through the most frequent causes, from the silliest to the subtlest.

## 1. The form is broken (or the messages go nowhere)

Start with the obvious, because it happens more often than you think: the form throws an error on submit, the captcha is broken, or the message gets sent… to an email address that no longer exists. The visitor does their part, hits "Send", and their message vanishes into the void. You never even know someone tried to reach you.

**How to fix it:** test it yourself. Open your site on your phone, fill in the form, and submit it. Check that the message arrives, which folder it lands in (check spam too), and how long it takes. If you use a form plugin, verify the destination address is still valid. It is a two-minute check that uncovers months-old problems.

## 2. Nobody can find how to contact you

The contact button buried in a submenu, the phone number only in the footer in small print, or service pages that end without any call to action. The visitor is interested… and has no idea what the next step is. Every extra click you ask of them is another chance for them to leave.

**How to fix it:** try the 30-second test. Open your site as if you were a stranger and time how long it takes you to find how to get in touch. If it takes you more than half a minute, your customer will not find it either. Put a clickable phone number in the header, a visible contact button on every page, and — even better — a short form at the end of your service pages, right where the visitor is already convinced.

## 3. The form asks for too much (or asks too soon)

Every field in your form is a small reason to give up. Industry data is blunt: only 38% of people who interact with a contact form actually submit it, and the average form abandonment rate sits around 68%. The field that creates the most friction is usually the phone number: nobody wants to hand their number to a business they just discovered, for fear of getting a sales call.

And note, it is not just about length. A long form with a clear reason gets completed; a short form with no reason gets abandoned just the same. The real problem is asking for data before the visitor sees what they get in return.

**How to fix it:** ask only for the essentials: name, email, and message. Make the phone number optional, never required. And right before the submit button, say what happens next: "We reply within one business day". When visitors know what to expect, filling in the form feels worth the effort.

## 4. Your site does not make clear what you offer or why they should pick you

"We are industry leaders", "quality and commitment", "integrated solutions". Copy that could belong on any company's website and says nothing at all. If within five seconds a visitor cannot tell what you do, who you do it for, and why they should choose you over the business next door, they leave and keep searching. And many websites talk about the company ("we, we, we") instead of talking about the customer's problems.

**How to fix it:** review your main headline. It should answer three questions: what you do, who you do it for, and what benefit they get. "We repair boilers in Los Barrios within 24 hours" converts better than "Welcome to our website". Speak your customer's language: their problems, their doubts, their urgencies. Not yours.

## 5. You give no reason to trust you

Put yourself in the visitor's shoes: they land on a website they have never seen and are asked for their personal details. No reviews, no real photos, no physical address, no "about us" page that puts a human face on the business. Asking for trust without giving anything back does not work: lack of trust is one of the main reasons people give up before making contact.

**How to fix it:** add visible social proof: real Google reviews, customer testimonials, genuine photos of the business and the team (not stock photos). Show your address and full contact details, and link your privacy policy next to the form. Anything that proves there are real people behind the website helps.

## 6. You take too long to reply

Sometimes the problem is not the website at all, but what happens afterwards. The message arrives on a Friday evening and you reply on Monday morning. By then, the customer has already called three competitors. The data is clear: 78% of buyers end up choosing the business that responds first. Response speed is not a detail: it is a competitive advantage.

**How to fix it:** turn on instant notifications on your phone for every form message. Aim to reply in minutes, not days. And set up an automatic confirmation message ("We have received your message, we will contact you today") so the visitor knows they did not write into the void. If you cannot reply instantly, make your phone or WhatsApp clearly visible as an immediate alternative.

## 7. The mobile experience fails

More than half of visits come from mobile, and that is exactly where most enquiries are lost: forms that are awkward to fill in with a thumb, buttons that are too small, unreadable text, or a site that is slow to load. A visitor arriving from Google on their phone who meets a clumsy website does not persist: they go back and try the next result.

**How to fix it:** repeat all the tests above from a phone, which is where your customers are. Big, well-spaced buttons, a form that works one-handed, and text that is readable without zooming. And if the site is slow to load, remember: speed is conversion too.

## In short: your checklist

If your website gets visits but nobody contacts you, work through these points in order:

1. Send yourself a test form submission and confirm the message arrives.
2. Do the 30-second test: is it easy to find how to contact you?
3. Shorten the form and explain what happens after sending it.
4. Clarify your message: what you do, who it is for, and why you.
5. Add trust signals: reviews, real photos, and complete details.
6. Reply fast: set up instant alerts and confirm receipt.
7. Test everything from a mobile phone.

A website is not measured by how pretty it is or what it cost to build. It is measured by the enquiries it generates. And when it generates none, the cause is almost always on this list.
