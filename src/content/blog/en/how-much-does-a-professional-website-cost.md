You ask for three quotes to build your website and get three completely different numbers: €600, €2,000 and €8,000. It looks like the same website in all three cases, so suspicion is inevitable: are they trying to overcharge me, or am I missing something by going cheap?

You are not alone. "How much does a website cost?" is one of the most searched questions by businesses and freelancers, and also one of the worst answered on the internet. In this guide you will find real 2026 prices, what each range includes, and how to decide which budget makes sense for your business.

## Real website prices in 2026

In Spain, a professional website costs between €500 and €8,000+ depending on the type of project. These are the realistic ranges by website type:

### Landing page: €350 – €900

A single page built around one action: generating leads, presenting a service or launching a product. Includes mobile-friendly design, a contact form and basic optimization. Ideal if you need a fast online presence with one very clear goal.

### Basic corporate website: €600 – €2,500

The most common choice for freelancers and small businesses: 4 to 10 pages (home, services, about, contact...), responsive design, contact form, Google Maps integration and basic on-page SEO. Usually delivered in 3 to 5 weeks.

### Premium corporate website: €1,500 – €4,000

Adds a blog, multiple languages, an SEO-oriented architecture, integrations (WhatsApp, social media, CRM) and more refined, often custom, design. This is the website you need if ranking on Google is part of your strategy.

### Online store: €1,500 – €8,000

An e-commerce needs a manageable catalog, a payment gateway, a shopping cart, shipping calculation, invoicing and an order dashboard, plus product-specific SEO. The price grows with the number of products and integrations.

## What determines the price of a website

Understanding what makes a project more expensive helps you read a quote critically. These are the highest-impact factors, from most to least:

### The type of website

This is the decisive factor. A landing page can double or multiply its cost by ten when it becomes an online store or a custom web application. Before requesting quotes, be clear about which type of website your goals require.

### Template-based vs custom design

A premium template adapted to your brand costs €300–€600; a design created from scratch starts around €1,500. Templates are faster and cheaper; custom design sets you apart and fits your business exactly.

### Features

Online booking, client areas, payments, CRM or ERP integrations... each feature adds development hours and can double the final price. Define what is essential and what can wait.

### Content

Copywriting, photography and video. If you provide the content yourself, the budget can drop by 20–30%. If you need professional copy and photo shoots, add that to the cost.

### Who builds the website

A junior freelancer charges €15–€30/hour; a specialized agency, €50–€90/hour. You are not just paying for hours: you are paying for experience, process and guarantees. A small specialized studio can deliver a professional website in a few days with an efficient workflow, while a large agency may take two to three months.

## The hidden costs nobody tells you about

The initial quote is not the total cost. These recurring expenses usually surprise first-time website owners:

### Domain: €10 – €15 per year

Your address on the internet (yourbusiness.com). Cheap, but essential and renewed yearly.

### Hosting: €60 – €300 per year

Where your website lives. Slow hosting ruins loading speed and, with it, your rankings and conversions. This is not the place to cut corners.

### SSL certificate

Mandatory today (the browser padlock), and most decent hosting providers include it for free. If they charge you extra for it, be suspicious.

### Maintenance: €30 – €100 per month

Updates, backups, small changes and support. A website without maintenance becomes outdated and vulnerable within months.

## Freelancer, agency, or DIY

### Doing it yourself

Platforms like Wix or the free WordPress plan let you build something basic without coding. But they come with strings attached: a subdomain instead of your own domain, platform advertising, generic templates and very limited SEO. For a business that wants to look professional, it is rarely the best option.

### Freelancer

Cheaper with direct communication. Perfect for clear, well-defined projects. The risk: if the freelancer disappears, you are left without support.

### Agency or studio

More expensive, but with a team, a process and continuity. The best option when the website is central to your business: online store, lead generation or brand image.

## How to choose the right budget

The right question is not "how much does a website cost?" but "which website do I need for my goals?". Use this practical rule:

- **You just need to exist online** (a digital business card): basic corporate website, €600 – €1,500.
- **You want to win clients from Google**: premium website with SEO from day one, €1,500 – €4,000.
- **You are going to sell online**: e-commerce, from €1,500 depending on catalog and integrations.
- **You need something unique** (booking, private areas, integrations): custom development, personalized quote.

Whatever the range, demand that the quote includes: your own domain, responsive design, optimized loading speed, basic on-page SEO and an HTTPS certificate. In 2026, that is not an extra: it is the minimum for a professional website.

## Conclusion

A professional website in 2026 costs between €500 and €8,000 depending on what you need. Be wary of quotes that do not break down what they include and of unbelievably low prices hiding monthly fees. With the ranges and factors in this guide, you can now request quotes knowing exactly what you are comparing... and which questions to ask before signing.
