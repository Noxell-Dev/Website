Your domain name is your business's address on the internet: what customers type into the browser, what appears on your business cards, and what Google ties to your brand. It is one of the few decisions that is expensive to undo — changing domains later means redirects, lost links, and confused customers.

So it deserves ten minutes of thought before you register it. In this guide you will see what makes a domain good, which extension to choose, the most common mistakes, and a checklist to register it with confidence.

## What a domain name is and why it matters so much

A domain name has two parts: the name you choose (`yourbusiness`) and the extension (`.com`, `.es`, `.net`...). Together they form your website's full address, like `yourbusiness.com`, and also the base of your professional email addresses (`hello@yourbusiness.com`).

A good domain does three things for you:

- **It is memorable.** A customer who hears it once should be able to type it without asking how it is spelled.
- **It builds trust.** A clean, own domain looks professional; a long one, full of hyphens or with an odd extension, raises doubts.
- **It grows with your brand.** It is the name you will repeat on invoices, social media, signs, and conversations for years.

The domain itself is not a shortcut to ranking on Google: what really ranks a site is content, links, and quality. But a confusing domain can cost you traffic, because people mistype it or forget it.

## How to choose the name: 7 rules that work

### 1. Keep it short

Try to stay under 15 characters, not counting the extension. Every extra letter is another chance to mistype it. Two words at most; three is already the practical limit, because nobody remembers the order.

- **Good:** `lunabakery.com`
- **Bad:** `artisan-bakery-luna-of-madrid.com`

### 2. Pass the phone test

Say the domain out loud to someone and ask them to type it without having seen it. If they have to ask how it is spelled, hesitate between two letters, or type it wrong, that domain will cost you customers. It is the cheapest and most reliable test there is.

### 3. No hyphens or numbers

Hyphens are impossible to communicate by voice ("dash") and people forget them when typing, so the traffic ends up at the hyphen-free domain — which may belong to someone else. Numbers have the same problem: is it "3" or "three"? "7" or "seven"? Unless the number is part of your brand (like 3M), avoid them.

### 4. Reflect your brand, not just what you sell

A domain with your company name still works if you change services, cities, or sectors. One built on keywords (`plumbing-malaga.com`) locks you into a niche and a city. If your brand already has a name, use it exactly as it is: not one letter more, not one less.

Keywords in a domain can help a little in making clear what you do, but they do not make up for a long or difficult name. A short, distinctive brand name always wins in the long run.

### 5. Easy to pronounce and spell

Avoid unnecessary double consonants, confusing letter combinations, and words in a language your audience does not speak. If your customers are Spanish-speaking, a domain in complicated English is a barrier, not a flourish.

And read the name with no spaces before you register it: some combinations spell words you do not want on your van or your business card.

### 6. Don't limit your future

Think about where you want to be in five years. A domain with a city name ties you to that city; one with a single product ties you to that product. If there is any chance you will grow, diversify, or sell elsewhere, choose a name that does not close doors.

### 7. It should sound good out loud

More and more people reach websites through voice search. If your domain sounds odd, gets confused with another word, or is hard to dictate to a voice assistant, you are losing visits you do not even know you are losing.

## Which extension to choose: .com, .es, and the rest

The extension is the part after the dot. These are the usual options for a business:

| Extension | When it fits | Keep in mind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **.com** | Almost any business; the default option | The best known, and the one people type out of habit; short names are nearly all taken |
| **.es** | Businesses aimed mainly at the Spanish market | Builds trust with local customers and helps Google associate your site with Spain |
| **.net** | A serious alternative when the .com is taken | Less intuitive than .com, but professional |
| **.org** | Associations, foundations, and non-profit projects | Not the best choice for a commercial business |
| **.shop, .store, .online...** | Shops or very specific projects | Useful when the name fits, but many people do not remember them and end up typing .com |

For a business in Spain, the ideal combination is often to register **.com** and **.es** together: use one as your main domain and redirect the other to it. That protects your brand and stops anyone from registering the other version to profit from your name.

A good name on a lesser-known extension is worth more than a bad name on .com. But if you can get a good name on .com, it is the safest bet.

## Before you register: four essential checks

### 1. Availability

Check it in any domain registrar's search tool. Do it before you fall in love with a name: the first five names that come to mind are also the first five that come to everyone else's mind.

If your exact name is taken, try variations before switching extensions: adding a short word related to your work (`yourbusinessstudio.com`), your city, or a verb (`getyourbusiness.com`) usually works better than a completely different name.

### 2. Trademarks

Search the name in your country's trademark database — in Spain, the Spanish Patent and Trademark Office (OEPM); for Europe, the EUIPO. If another company already holds that name as a trademark in your sector, it can claim the domain and force you to change it. Five minutes now can save you a full rebrand.

### 3. Social media

Check that the name is free, or at least available in a consistent variation, on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, and the networks you plan to use. If every version is taken by active accounts, that is a sign of conflict and branding headaches ahead.

### 4. Domain history

If the domain had a previous owner, it is worth checking its past. A domain once used for spam or dubious content can carry a bad reputation with Google. Free tools like the Wayback Machine show you what used to be at that address.

## How much a domain costs and what to check when registering

A standard `.com` or `.es` domain costs between €10 and €20 a year. The first-year price is usually lower thanks to promotions, so look mainly at the **renewal price**: that is what you will pay every year for the life of the domain. Some names the registrar considers "premium" can cost hundreds or thousands of euros; in that case, it is almost always worth finding another name.

When you register, keep this in mind:

- **Register it in your own name.** The domain must be under your company name or yours — never under the designer's, the registrar's, or an intermediary's name. It is your asset.
- **Turn on auto-renewal.** Losing a domain over an unread email is more common than it sounds, and getting it back can be expensive or impossible.
- **Turn on WHOIS privacy.** It hides your personal details from the public domain database and cuts down spam. Many registrars include it for free.
- **Enable transfer lock and two-factor authentication** on your registrar account. That is how you stop anyone from stealing or transferring your domain.
- **Register it for several years** if you are sure the project is serious: you lock in today's price and signal stability.

## Common mistakes when choosing a domain

- **Copying a competitor's name** with one letter changed. It confuses your customers and can bring legal trouble.
- **Choosing a trendy name** with slang or abbreviations nobody will understand in two years.
- **Registering only one variant** and letting someone else take the .es or .com of your brand.
- **Leaving registration in someone else's hands** without checking the domain is in your name.
- **Looking only at the first-year price** and getting a surprise at renewal.
- **Changing domains lightly** once you already have a website, emails, and customers using the current one: the switch costs links, rankings, and a lot of work.

## Checklist before you pay

- Under 15 characters and at most two or three words.
- Passes the phone test: someone types it correctly after hearing it once.
- No hyphens, numbers, or creative spellings.
- Matches your brand name, or comes very close to it.
- Does not limit you if you grow, move city, or add services.
- The trademark is not held by another company in your sector.
- The name is free (or consistent) on social media.
- You checked the renewal price, not just the first-year price.
- You will register it in your own name, with auto-renewal and WHOIS privacy.

If your candidate ticks every box, register it today. A good domain does not get better by waiting: while you think about it, someone else can register it.
