More and more people ask ChatGPT, Perplexity or Gemini before they ask Google. And when the AI answers, it does not show a list of ten links: it writes a single answer and names a handful of businesses. If your business is not among them, for that person you do not exist.

That shift is what GEO is about: positioning in AI search engines. This guide explains what it is, how it differs from classic SEO, and what you can do so that AI answers talk about your business.

## What is GEO?

GEO stands for *generative engine optimization*. It is the work of getting AI systems that write answers — ChatGPT with search, Perplexity, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, and Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode — to find your content, understand it, trust it and cite it when they answer a question.

Classic SEO tries to get your page into the results list so the user clicks through. GEO tries to get your business inside the answer the AI writes, even if the user never clicks a single link.

The term comes from an academic paper published in November 2023 by researchers from Princeton and IIT Delhi, presented at the KDD 2024 conference. The authors built a benchmark of 10,000 real queries and found that certain content changes could raise a source's visibility in AI answers by up to 40%. The tactics that worked best were adding statistics, citing reliable sources and including direct quotes from experts.

## How GEO differs from SEO

They share the same foundation, but the goal is completely different.

| Aspect | SEO | GEO |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goal | Rank a page in the results | Get cited inside the AI's answer |
| Where you appear | Google or Bing link lists | Answers from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, AI Overviews |
| What the user does | Scans links and clicks | Reads the answer, and may not click at all |
| What gets picked | The page | The passage that best answers the question |
| How it is measured | Rankings, impressions, clicks | Mentions and citations of your brand in AI answers |

GEO does not replace SEO. It is a layer on top of it: most AI engines search the web before they answer, so they can only cite pages that can be crawled and indexed. SEO gets your page into the candidate set; GEO decides whether it ends up cited.

Google says so itself in its own guide: from its point of view, optimizing for its generative AI features is still SEO. There are no separate technical requirements for appearing in AI Overviews or AI Mode: the page needs to be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet, just like in normal search.

## How AI engines choose which sources to cite

No company publishes its full formula, but what Google, Microsoft and OpenAI have explained publicly describes a similar process:

1. **The question is expanded.** Google explains that its AI features may use *query fan-out*: running several related searches across different subtopics to build a single answer.
2. **Candidate pages are retrieved.** The systems query their index and pull the relevant pages, a process Google calls *retrieval-augmented generation* or *grounding*. ChatGPT uses its own crawler, OAI-SearchBot, alongside Microsoft search technology.
3. **The best passages are selected.** Microsoft explains that assistants such as Copilot break pages into smaller pieces and evaluate each one for authority and relevance.
4. **The answer is written and sources are cited.** The model writes a single answer and names the sources it relied on.

Three things tip the balance when it comes to citations: clear structure, with short passages that answer one specific question; consensus, meaning several independent sources saying the same thing; and freshness, content that was published or updated recently.

One figure says it all: according to Ahrefs data published in March 2026, only 38% of AI Overview citations came from pages in the organic top 10, down from 76% in July 2025. Ranking well no longer guarantees being cited.

## Does GEO affect your business? Yes, and more every day

The habit shift is already underway. In BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026, 45% of US consumers said they had used an AI tool to find local business recommendations in the past 12 months, up from just 6% the year before.

Think about what that means for a small business: a potential customer asks "what is the best café in my neighbourhood?" or "who installs air conditioning near me?" and the AI answers by naming two or three names. If yours is not there, you do not just lose a click: you never even enter the conversation.

There is another important nuance: in GEO you do not compete page against page for a keyword, but brand against brand for a recommendation. The AI builds its picture of your business from many places at once: your website, review platforms, directories, news coverage and mentions in other media.

## What you can do to appear in AI answers

You do not need expensive tools or to be an expert. These are the actions that matter most, ordered from the most basic to the most advanced.

### 1. Let the AI crawlers in

Check that your `robots.txt` does not block AI crawlers such as OpenAI's GPTBot and OAI-SearchBot or PerplexityBot. If they cannot read your site, they cannot cite it. And keep in mind that many of these crawlers read raw HTML only, without running JavaScript the way Googlebot does, so the important content needs to be in the page's HTML, not loaded by scripts alone.

### 2. Write direct, quotable answers

Every page should answer one specific question in a short, self-contained passage that still makes sense when lifted out of context. Start the page or section with the direct answer and expand on it afterwards. Add a FAQ section with clear answers: it is exactly the format AI systems copy most easily.

### 3. Back up what you say with data and sources

The original GEO study made it clear: pages with statistics, quotes from reliable sources and expert statements were the ones that gained the most visibility in AI answers. Instead of writing "we are the best in our industry", write concrete figures with their source: years of experience, number of customers, measurable results. And link to the original source when you use a figure that is not your own.

### 4. Use structured data

Add Schema.org markup to your pages: `Organization`, `LocalBusiness`, `FAQPage`, `Article` or `Product`, depending on the case. It is the most direct way to tell a machine what your business is, where it is, what it offers and what its contact details are. Without structured data, the AI has to guess all of that from the text.

### 5. Keep your details consistent everywhere

AI systems cross-check information from your website, your Google Business Profile, directories and review platforms. If your hours, phone number or services do not match across those places, the AI will not know which figure is correct and will probably not cite you. Keep your name, address, phone number and hours identical everywhere, and ask happy customers to leave reviews: they are a very strong trust signal.

### 6. Get others talking about you

Mentions in media, industry blogs, directories and review platforms carry a lot of weight in GEO, because the AI trusts what others say about you more than what you say about yourself. You do not need to appear in a national newspaper: a mention in an industry blog, a local guide or a trusted directory already helps.

### 7. Update your content regularly

Recent content gets cited more. Review your most important pages every few months: update figures, add news and fix anything that has gone out of date. A page with a visible update date signals freshness and reliability.

## How to tell if it is working

GEO has no single official dashboard like SEO does, so measurement is more hands-on. For a small business, the most practical approach is:

- **Ask the AIs directly.** Put the questions your customers would ask to ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini ("best plumber in my city", "bike shop near me") and note whether your business appears and what it says about you. Repeat the test over several weeks and watch the trend, not a single answer, because AI answers change from one day to the next.
- **Check Search Console.** Google added a generative AI performance report to Search Console, rolled out to all websites in August 2026, which shows impressions coming from AI Overviews and AI Mode.
- **Look at referral traffic.** In your analytics, watch for visits coming from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai or Copilot: those are people who found you through an AI answer.

## What does not work in GEO

Two common mistakes are worth avoiding. The first is thinking there is a magic file or a technical trick that solves everything: Google has said no `llms.txt` file is needed to appear in its AI features. The second is keyword stuffing, which in the original study improved nothing: what works is clear content, with real data and cited sources, not repeating the keyword twenty times.

## Conclusion

Search is changing: more and more people ask an AI and read an answer, instead of scanning a list of links. GEO is the work of making sure your business is inside that answer. It does not replace SEO, it completes it: a healthy website, with clear content, verifiable data and a good reputation elsewhere, is the one most likely to get cited.

Start with the basics: let the crawlers in, write direct answers, use real data with its sources, and keep your information up to date everywhere. They are small steps, but they are the ones that decide whether, the next time someone asks about what you do, the AI answers with your name.
