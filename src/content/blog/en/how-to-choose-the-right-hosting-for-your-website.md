Your website is finished, it looks great… and now someone tells you that you still need to buy hosting. Suddenly words like "shared", "VPS", "cloud" or "dedicated" start flying around, prices range from €3 to €100 a month, and you get the feeling that if you pick the wrong one, your site will run slowly or go down at the worst possible moment.

Choosing hosting is one of the decisions that affects your website the most: it shapes its speed, whether it stays online when someone visits, and what you will pay in the long run. The good news is that you do not need to be technical to choose well — you just need to understand which types exist and what to check before signing up.

## What is web hosting?

Web hosting is the service that stores your website's files on a computer that is always on — a server — connected to the internet. When someone types your address into their browser, that server sends them your website.

Without hosting there is no visible website: you can have the design finished, but if it is not stored on any server, nobody can see it. That is why hosting is not a detail — it is the foundation everything else runs on.

## The types of hosting

Providers use many different marketing names, but almost every plan falls into a few types. The main difference is whether the server's resources (processor, memory and disk) are shared with other customers or reserved for you, and how much technical work falls on you.

### Shared hosting

Your website lives on a server alongside other customers' websites. The provider updates the system, watches over security and gives you a panel (cPanel, hPanel or their own) to upload files, create email accounts and install WordPress in one click.

It is the cheapest option: plans usually cost between €3 and €10 a month, although the renewal price can be up to five times the introductory price. It is enough for a business website, a blog or a company page with moderate traffic.

Its limit is that resources are shared: if a neighbouring website gets a traffic spike, yours can slow down or show errors. Each account also has a processor and memory cap that is not always advertised clearly.

### Managed WordPress hosting

In most cases this is still shared infrastructure, but optimised for a single application: WordPress. The provider handles updates, backups and part of the security, and the panel is designed to manage the site without touching anything technical.

It suits you if your site is built with WordPress and you want to forget about maintenance. In return, you have less freedom to install other things and the price is a bit higher than basic shared hosting.

### VPS (virtual private server)

A VPS is a slice of a server with its processor, memory and disk reserved just for you. You can install whatever you like and nobody else uses your resources.

Unmanaged plans start at around €4 a month, and managed ones — where a technician from the provider handles maintenance — start at around €50 a month. Most VPS plans are sold unmanaged: the provider keeps the machine running and you take care of everything inside, including updates, security and backups.

It makes sense for online stores with heavy traffic, database-driven applications or tools that need their own environment. It is not the best first choice if you have no technical knowledge or nobody to manage it.

### Cloud hosting

The word "cloud" is used for two different things. Some providers call "cloud hosting" a shared plan with more resources and fewer neighbours. And then there are services that copy your website across servers around the world and serve it from the one closest to the visitor, like Cloudflare Pages or AWS Amplify.

The latter only work for static websites — the ones that do not generate each page on the spot — but they are the fastest and cheapest option: they can cost just a few cents a month. This is the choice many studios make for the company pages they build from scratch, without WordPress.

### Dedicated server

A whole physical machine rented for a single customer. It is the most powerful option and also the most expensive, and it needs to be managed entirely. Only large projects or those with very specific requirements need one. For the vast majority of businesses, it is paying for more than you will ever use.

## What to check before signing up for hosting

The advertised price is the first thing people look at, but it is not what matters most. Here is what really makes the difference:

### Speed and real resources

Do not look only at disk space: what really matters is the processor, the RAM and the process limits. Look for SSD or NVMe disks and a modern web server (LiteSpeed, Nginx or a well-configured Apache). Server location matters too: from Spain, a server in Paris responds much sooner than one in the United States, and that difference shows on every visit.

### Uptime

Uptime is the percentage of time your website is online. 99.9% sounds perfect, but it still means around 8 hours and 45 minutes of downtime a year. 99% is already more than three and a half days. Check the guarantee the provider offers and whether they compensate you for outages.

### Backups

Ask whether backups are daily and automatic, whether they are stored away from the server and how easy they are to restore. A website without backups is a website that can vanish overnight because of a mistake, an attack or a provider failure.

### SSL certificate included

SSL is what puts the padlock in the browser and makes your address start with `https://`. Today it is essential: without it, browsers warn that the site is not secure and Google penalises it. Almost every provider includes it for free with Let's Encrypt — if they charge extra for it, be suspicious.

### Support that actually answers

When your website goes down, what matters is that someone helps you quickly and understands you. Check whether support is in your language, through which channels (chat, phone, ticket) and during which hours. Read real customer reviews before deciding: slow support turns any small problem into a whole day without a website.

### The renewal price

Many providers sell the first year at €2 or €3 a month and raise it to €8 or €10 on renewal. Before signing up, look for the renewal price: that is what you will pay every year after the first. A cheap first-year plan can be the most expensive one after three years.

### Room to grow without moving

Your website today will not be the same in two years. Choose a provider that lets you upgrade your plan without migrating the site elsewhere, and that does not charge you for switching plans. Changing hosting halfway is possible, but it is always a hassle you can avoid.

## Which hosting does your website need?

There is no best hosting in the abstract: there is the right one for your type of website, your traffic and your team.

- **Company page or blog with few visits:** good shared hosting or managed WordPress. If the site is static and does not use WordPress, cloud hosting is even faster and cheaper.
- **Online store with regular sales:** a managed VPS or a cloud plan with reserved resources is better, especially if you run ad campaigns that bring traffic spikes.
- **Custom application or internal tool:** you need control over the environment: VPS, cloud or containers, depending on the architecture.
- **Large project with heavy traffic or special requirements:** a dedicated server or a well-sized cloud infrastructure.

If you are torn between two options, start with the simpler one: it is easier to upgrade when the site grows than to pay from day one for resources you do not use.

## Common mistakes when choosing hosting

- **Choosing only by the lowest price.** The cheapest plan usually has the most crowded servers and the worst support.
- **Ignoring the renewal price.** The first year is not representative: work out what you will pay from the second year on.
- **Buying a VPS without knowing how to manage it.** An unpatched, unprotected server is a security problem waiting to happen.
- **Not checking where the server is.** A server far from your customers adds fractions of a second to every page load.
- **Not asking about backups.** Finding out they do not exist when you already need them is too late.
- **Choosing a plan with no room to grow.** If your site takes off and the hosting cannot cope, the move comes at the worst moment.

## Checklist for choosing well

1. Define what kind of website you have and how much traffic you expect.
2. Pick the hosting type that fits that — not the most powerful one, nor the cheapest.
3. Compare the renewal price, not the introductory one.
4. Check that free SSL and daily automatic backups are included.
5. Make sure the server is close to your visitors.
6. Check that support is in your language and answers quickly.
7. Confirm you can upgrade your plan without migrating the site.

Choosing the right hosting is not about finding the perfect plan — it is about avoiding the wrong one: one that lets you down when your website needs it most. With the right type, automatic backups and support that answers, your website will be in good hands from day one.
