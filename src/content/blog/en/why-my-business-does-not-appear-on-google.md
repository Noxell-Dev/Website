You search for your business name on Google and it doesn't show up. Not in the results, not on Maps. Meanwhile, your competitors are there, getting the calls and visits that could be yours.

It's one of the most frustrating problems for a local business, but the good news is that it almost always has a specific cause... and a fix. Let's go through them, from the most common to the most technical.

## 1. You have no Google Business Profile (or it's unverified)

For local searches, your Google Business Profile is as important as your website. If it doesn't exist, Google simply has no listing to show on Maps or in the side panel of results.

**How to fix it:** create your profile at google.com/business, add your real address, choose the right category, and complete verification (usually by video or postcard). It's the single highest-impact action you can take.

## 2. Your profile exists but is incomplete

A profile with no photos, no hours, no description and no reviews is, to Google, almost the same as having no profile. The search engine prioritizes businesses with complete, up-to-date information.

**How to fix it:** fill in every field: primary and secondary categories, hours (including holidays), real photos of your premises and your work, a description of your services and area. A complete profile can rank among the top three local results in your industry.

## 3. You have duplicate listings

It happens more often than you'd think: someone created a profile for your company years ago, you changed name or address, or an agency managed the profile from another account. Google may show the old listing... or none at all, treating them as duplicates.

**How to fix it:** search for your business by name and by address on Maps. If two similar listings appear, request a merge or removal of the duplicate from the management dashboard. And keep your data (name, address, phone) consistent across your website and every directory: inconsistencies confuse Google.

## 4. Your website isn't indexed by Google

If not even your own site appears when searching `site:yourdomain.com`, the problem is indexing: Google doesn't know your website exists or can't crawl it.

**How to fix it:** open Google Search Console, paste your URL into "URL Inspection" and check the status. The most common culprits are a `robots.txt` blocking crawlers, a `noindex` tag forgotten after development, or a sitemap that was never submitted. Each status tells you exactly where the problem is.

## 5. You're indexed, but not ranking

Google knows your website exists, but it sits on page 5 for the searches that matter to you. This is no longer a technical problem: it's a ranking problem. Typical causes are thin or generic content, a website that never explicitly mentions your city or your services, and lack of authority (nobody links to your site).

**How to fix it:** mention your location and services in your copy, page titles and meta descriptions. Publish useful content that answers your customers' real questions. And earn mentions: industry directories, suppliers, local media.

## 6. You have few reviews (or none)

Reviews are one of the strongest trust signals for local SEO. A business with 40 positive ratings is far more likely to appear than one with none, even if they offer exactly the same thing.

**How to fix it:** ask for reviews systematically. Satisfied customers rarely leave them on their own: send them the direct link after every job well done, and reply to all of them — including the negative ones — politely.

## 7. Your website is slow or broken on mobile

Google directly penalizes pages that take too long to load or look bad on a phone. And it's not a whim: over 60% of local searches happen on mobile. If your site takes more than three seconds to load, you're losing rankings without knowing it.

**How to fix it:** test your site with PageSpeed Insights and tackle the basics: oversized images, slow hosting, unnecessary scripts. Speed is one of the few ranking factors you can improve in a single afternoon.

## 8. Your domain is very new

Domains younger than six months naturally carry less authority with Google. It's not a permanent problem, but it means patience and consistency: publish, earn reviews and keep your profile active while authority builds.

## How to diagnose it in 10 minutes

If you don't know where to start, follow this order:

1. Search your business by name + city in incognito mode. Do you appear?
2. Check your Business Profile: verified and complete?
3. Search `site:yourdomain.com`: is your website indexed?
4. Check Google Search Console: crawl errors or manual actions?
5. Count your reviews and compare them with your direct competitors.

In most cases, the problem is in the first three steps and gets solved in days, not months.

## Conclusion

Your business missing from Google is almost never bad luck: it's an unverified profile, a non-indexed website, missing reviews, or content that never clearly states what you do or where. Go through the eight causes in this guide in order and tackle the first one you find. The visibility your competitors enjoy today can be yours with far less effort than you imagine.
