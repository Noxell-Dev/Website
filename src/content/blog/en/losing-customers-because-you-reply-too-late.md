A customer messages you on WhatsApp at 11:00 on a Tuesday asking for a quote. You are on a job, in a meeting or with another customer, and you do not see it until 18:00. You reply helpfully and in good faith… and nobody answers. The next day you find out they hired someone else.

You did not lose that customer on price or quality. You lost them during the seven hours when your business was silent. And it is not a one-off: it is the most common pattern for small businesses handling enquiries through WhatsApp, Instagram, email or a website contact form.

## Why replying late costs you customers

When someone is looking for a service, they rarely message just one business. They message two or three at once, compare who replies first and keep talking to whoever gives them a useful answer earliest. Your competitor does not have to be better: they only have to be available sooner.

Research on response times has confirmed this for years:

- An audit published in Harvard Business Review covering 2,241 companies found an average response time of 42 hours, and 23% of companies never replied at all. Those that responded within the first hour were up to 7 times more likely to have a meaningful conversation with that prospect.
- The InsideSales and MIT study of thousands of web leads found that reaching out within the first 5 minutes, rather than after 30 minutes, made a lead 21 times more likely to move forward.
- In Drift's test of 433 companies, only 7% replied within 5 minutes, and more than half took longer than five business days to respond.

You do not need to reply in 30 seconds every time. But there is a huge difference between replying in 10 minutes and replying the next day. In the first case you are still in the conversation; in the second, the conversation has already ended with someone else.

### The problem is not just office hours

Many enquiries arrive exactly when you cannot handle them: at night, at the weekend, at peak times or while you are with another customer. If your system depends on you looking at your phone at the exact right moment, those messages are at a disadvantage from the start. The answer is not to live glued to your phone, but to build a system that replies even when you are not there.

## First, measure how long you actually take

Before changing anything, run a simple test over one week:

1. Look back at your last 20 conversations on WhatsApp, Instagram and email.
2. Note how long passed between the customer's first message and your first reply.
3. Count how many conversations got no reply at all, or went cold after your first answer.

Most businesses discover two things: they take much longer than they thought, and messages do not get lost in one place but scattered across channels nobody checks equally often. That diagnosis tells you where to act first.

## 1. Bring every message into one place

If enquiries come in through WhatsApp, Instagram direct messages, Facebook, email and your website form, and each channel is checked by a different person (or nobody), there will always be a corner where a message ages unseen.

**How to fix it:** choose one main channel and turn the others into entry points that lead to it. For example, link your Instagram and your website to the business WhatsApp number, bring email into a single inbox you actually check, and switch off channels you are not going to serve. It is better to serve two channels well than to promise five and fail on three. If several people are involved, use a shared inbox or a customer messaging tool that shows which messages are pending and who is handling them, so none falls through the cracks.

## 2. Make sure no message goes unnoticed

Many messages are lost for something as simple as a muted notification. The message arrived on time; nobody was told.

**How to fix it:** turn on notifications for your main channel on the phone of the person responsible, and check the app is not being delayed by battery-saving settings. If you have a team, set clear shifts: who replies in the morning, who in the afternoon and who covers weekends. And write down one simple rule, for example: "no message waits more than one hour for a first reply during working hours." Without an owner and a rule, fast replies depend on daily goodwill, and that fails precisely on the busiest days.

## 3. Turn on an automatic reply that actually helps

An automatic reply does not replace your answer: it buys you time. Its job is to tell the customer "we have seen your message, we exist, and we will reply by this time," so they do not go looking elsewhere while they wait.

### On WhatsApp Business it is free and takes minutes

If you still use regular WhatsApp for your business, switch to WhatsApp Business, which is free. Under **Business tools** you will find two key options:

- **Greeting message:** sent automatically when someone messages you for the first time or after 14 days without contact.
- **Away message:** sent when people message you outside your business hours, on a custom schedule, or whenever you turn it on. To use the outside-hours option, set your opening hours in your business profile first.

The same idea exists, under other names, in email (automatic or out-of-office replies) and on many social networks.

### What a good automatic reply should say

A good away message does three things: it confirms the message arrived, it says when you will reply, and it offers a way out for urgent cases. For example:

> "Hi, thanks for messaging us. We are currently outside our opening hours (Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 18:00), but we will reply as soon as we are back. If it is urgent, call us on [phone number]."

Avoid messages that say nothing ("Thanks for contacting us") or that promise what you will not deliver ("We reply within 5 minutes" if that is not true). A broken promise angers people more than an announced wait.

## 4. Prepare quick replies for what you are always asked

A large part of your response time is not thinking time: it is typing the same thing again and again — rough prices, opening hours, your service area, how to book, payment options. Every time you type that answer from scratch, you are slower, and you answer worse when you are in a hurry.

**How to fix it:** create quick replies. In WhatsApp Business they are under **Business tools > Quick replies**: you write the text once, assign a shortcut such as `/hours` or `/prices`, and afterwards you insert it in two taps. Prepare the five or six questions you get most often and leave the ending open so you can personalise each message slightly. The template makes you faster; the personal touch stops you sounding like a robot.

## 5. After hours, let an assistant handle the simple questions

There is a limit to what away messages can do: they confirm, but they do not answer. If a customer asks at 22:00 whether you open on Saturdays or what a specific service costs, they will have to wait until the next morning even though the answer is one sentence.

That is where a chatbot or an AI assistant on your website or WhatsApp can handle frequently asked questions outside working hours: opening hours, services, guide prices, how to book. Set up well, it answers instantly what has a clear answer and hands the conversation over to you when the question is complex, sensitive, or the customer asks for a person.

### When automation is enough and when you need a human

Automate what is repetitive and predictable. Keep people for tailored quotes, complaints, urgent cases and any conversation where the customer is about to decide. And check from time to time which questions the assistant could not answer: they are almost always information the business does have, which only needs to be added once so it does not happen again.

## 6. State how long you take to reply… and keep to it

If your website, Google profile or Instagram says nothing about response times, customers assume the worst after an hour of silence. A visible, realistic commitment ("We reply within 2 working hours") does two things instead: it reassures the person messaging you, and it obliges you to meet it.

**How to fix it:** pick a timeframe you can meet even in your worst weeks, not just your best ones, and publish it where people contact you. It is better to promise two hours and reply in 20 minutes than to promise 10 minutes and fail half the time.

## 7. Follow up before you write the conversation off

Replying fast is pointless if the conversation dies after your first answer. The customer asked, you replied with the price, and they never said anything again. Often they have not chosen someone else: they got distracted, left it for later, or had a question they did not dare to ask.

**How to fix it:** if a conversation is left open, send one short, pressure-free follow-up the next day: "Hi [name], did you get a chance to look at what I sent? If you have any questions, I can answer them here." A single kind, specific follow-up recovers some of those conversations. Five pushy messages, on the other hand, achieve the opposite.

## Your plan for this week

You do not need a perfect system; you need to stop losing messages. In order:

1. Measure your real response time from the past week.
2. Choose one main channel and direct the rest of your enquiries there.
3. Turn on notifications and name an owner (and a backup).
4. Set up the greeting message and the away message in WhatsApp Business.
5. Create quick replies for your five most frequent questions.
6. Publish a response timeframe you can actually meet.
7. Check every morning whether any conversation from yesterday is still unclosed.

Replying on time is not about holding your phone all day. It is about having a simple system that makes sure no customer is left talking to themselves, even when you are busy doing your job.
