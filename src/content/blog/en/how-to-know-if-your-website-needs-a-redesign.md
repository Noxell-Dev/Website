Your website has been with you for years. You launched it with enthusiasm, but lately something doesn't add up: you get visits and no one calls, it's a pain to update, and next to your competitors it looks like it's from another era.

The question isn't whether your website "works". The question is whether it's costing you customers. These are the 9 signs that it's time to redesign it.

## 1. It takes more than 3 seconds to load

Speed is the number-one sign. According to Google's research, **53% of mobile users abandon a page that takes more than 3 seconds to load**. And it's not just about patience: pages that load in 2 seconds have an average bounce rate of 9%, while those that take 5 seconds jump to 38%.

**How to fix it:** measure your site with PageSpeed Insights or Lighthouse and tackle the basics: unoptimized images, slow hosting and unnecessary scripts. Speed is one of the few ranking factors you can improve in a single afternoon.

## 2. It looks bad (or breaks) on mobile

More than half of global web traffic comes from mobile, and Google indexes pages with a mobile-first mindset. If on your phone you have to pinch to zoom, the menu gets cut off or buttons are impossible to tap, your website is driving customers away every day.

**How to fix it:** open your site on your own phone and ask two people to try to contact you from it. If it takes them more than a minute to find the call button or the form, you have a usability problem, not an aesthetics problem.

## 3. You get visits but no one contacts you

This is the quietest sign and the most expensive one. If Google Analytics shows traffic but the phone doesn't ring and no forms arrive, your website isn't converting. The typical causes: hidden contact buttons, endless forms, confusing messages and no social proof.

**How to fix it:** place a call button or a visible form above the fold on every important page. A single clear call to action per page can double your inquiries.

## 4. Your design sends the wrong signal

Users judge your business in seconds based on how your website looks. Outdated fonts, generic stock photos, colors that don't represent your brand... All of it says "this business hasn't kept up" before they read a single word.

**How to fix it:** you don't need the trendiest design in the world, you need consistency: real photos of your business, readable typography and a style that matches how your customers see you in person.

## 5. Your business changed but your website didn't

New services, new branding, new photos, new prices... If your website still tells the story from 5 years ago, it's selling a business that no longer exists. This is especially serious if your value proposition has changed: customers arrive with one expectation and find another.

**How to fix it:** review every section and ask "is this still true?". Everything that doesn't reflect your current business should go in the redesign.

## 6. Your competitors look more professional

Do the test: search for your services on Google and visit the 3–5 websites that rank above you. If theirs inspires more trust, has better photos and is easier to use, your customers noticed before you did. Comparison is inevitable: users choose among all of them.

**How to fix it:** don't copy their design, copy their standard. Note what they do well (clarity, photos, reviews, speed) and demand the same level from your own site.

## 7. Updating it is a nightmare

If changing a text, a photo or a price requires calling someone, waiting days and praying nothing breaks, your website is built on outdated technology. A modern website should be easy to manage: you should be able to publish changes yourself in minutes.

**How to fix it:** in the redesign, demand a system you can handle without help for day-to-day updates. Total dependence on third parties for minimal changes is a permanent hidden cost.

## 8. Google ignores you

If you publish content but don't show up for the searches that matter to you, part of the problem is often the website itself: duplicate titles, chaotic heading structure, missing structured data, illogical URLs. An old website is rarely ready for today's SEO.

**How to fix it:** run a quick audit with Google Search Console: how many pages are indexed? Are there crawl errors? A redesign is the perfect occasion to rebuild SEO from scratch, with proper redirects so you don't lose the traffic you already have.

## 9. You're embarrassed to share your web address

The definitive sign. If you avoid giving your URL to a potential customer, if you'd rather they call you before seeing it, if you wince every time someone says "I'll take a look at your website"... you already have your answer.

**How to fix it:** stop patching and plan the redesign for what it is: an investment, not an expense. A website you're embarrassed about is holding back every sales conversation you have.

## Redesign or small tweaks: how to decide

Not everything requires starting from scratch. Use this rule:

- **Full redesign** if you match 4 or more signs on this list, especially speed, mobile and conversion.
- **Tactical update** if the problem is only content, photos or calls to action, and the technical foundation is solid.
- **Ongoing optimization** if your site is fast, looks good on mobile and converts: then it just needs maintenance.

Before you start, measure: record your speed, your bounce rate and your current inquiries. Without a baseline you won't know whether the redesign was worth it.

## Conclusion

Your website isn't a static poster: it's your best salesperson, working 24 hours a day. If it's slow to load, breaks on mobile, generates no inquiries or you're embarrassed to show it, it's not doing its job. Go through the 9 signs, count how many you match and act accordingly. The sooner you do, the sooner it stops costing you customers.
